# 3d

The 3D renderer: the components that name what an entity draws — a mesh or a
surface of elements — the system that turns those tables into draws with a
camera and a sun, the two matrices an object is drawn with, the order
see-through things have to be drawn in, and the step that reads a model into a
world. The only folder that may name `render`, `scene`,
`ecs` and `assets` at once, which is why the import lives here.

Nothing here names Vulkan. A mesh is an id, a material is factors and ids, and
a gap in what `render` can do is filled in `render` — never reached around.

- `include/3d/projection.h` — a field of view into a projection matrix. Its
  header says why this is neither `math`'s nor `scene`'s, and which three files
  have to agree about depth running backwards.
- `include/3d/mesh_component.h` — what an entity draws, as a range in `render`'s
  pools, and which of the two layers it draws in. Its header says why it holds an
  id and not a buffer, why the layer is a property of the drawable rather than of
  the material or the transform, why neither layer is the normal case, why the
  table is written directly rather than through a system and what would change
  that, and why the geometry is the one field that changes after creation.
- `include/3d/panel_component.h` — what an entity draws when it draws a surface
  of elements: a range of this frame's element buffer, the surface's size in
  millimetres and which layer it is in. Its header says why the range is one
  frame's and wrong the moment that frame has ended, why a panel is a component
  and not an entity, why it holds no matrix and which four factors the draw
  system composes instead, why the size is here rather than read off the
  elements, that a millimetre on a panel is a real millimetre and where the other
  meaning of the word lives, why there is no panel system, and why the range is
  the only field that changes after creation.
- `include/3d/material_component.h` — the factors and texture ids a surface is
  shaded with, its alpha mode, whether it is lit at all, whether its base colour
  texture is a distance field rather than a picture, which rectangle of that
  texture it reads, and the record `render` holds for them. Its header says which
  four channels are shaded with and which two are only stored, where the
  deduplication of a shared picture comes from, why the alpha mode is `render`'s
  enum rather than a second one, why neither the unlit flag nor the
  distance-field one is something the importer ever sets, why the rectangle is
  the base colour's alone, and why a scale of nothing in it means the whole
  texture.
- `include/3d/normal_matrix.h` — a world matrix into the matrix its normals
  want. Its header says why a normal cannot be carried by the world matrix, what
  that looks like on screen, and why this is neither `math`'s nor `render`'s.
- `include/3d/depth_sort.h` — the order see-through objects have to be drawn
  in. Its header says why blending is not commutative, why the key is one point
  per object, and which way round "further away" is.
- `include/3d/draw_system.h` — the world drawn into the pass the loop has
  opened, and the camera and sun worked out once for it. Its header says why the
  loop owns the frame and how its phases are ordered, why the camera is computed
  before the pass and handed back, why a begin with nothing to draw into is the
  loop's case, why the run returns nothing, what the
  draw loop deliberately does not do, which pass an entity's alpha mode puts it
  in and why table order is safe for one of them and not the other, how the two
  layers run across those two passes with one depth clear between them, why the
  layer is a clear rather than the depth test turned off, why it decides order
  and never lighting, which three components an entity needs before it is drawn,
  why it wants an arena, and why a world needs exactly one camera and exactly one
  sun.
- `include/3d/import.h` — a `.glb`'s bytes into entities. Its header says which
  folder each step of the pipeline belongs to, where the node tree is flattened,
  and the one thing decomposing a world matrix loses.
- `src/projection.c` — the arithmetic, written out, because the signs are the
  whole thing.
- `src/normal_matrix.c` — the inverse transpose, the derivation in three lines,
  and the one branch a flattened object needs.
- `src/mesh_component.c` — the key, the creation call, the geometry change and
  the reads.
- `src/panel_component.c` — the same, with the range in place of the geometry.
- `src/material_component.c` — the same, plus the upload that turns a
  material's numbers into a record. Its header says why the UV rect is the one
  field that is not a straight copy.
- `src/depth_sort.c` — the insertion sort, and the sign is the whole of it.
- `src/draw_system.c` — the camera, the sun, two matrices per object, the solid
  pass in table order and the blended one furthest first, on each side of the
  overlay's depth clear, over both tables. Its header says why there are four
  groups, why only one of them can be drawn as a table is walked, why the order
  of the two walks decides nothing, why the overlay has a solid group from the
  start, and why an empty overlay clears nothing. `struct deferred`'s comment
  says why a held-back entry is one of two things, and `range_is_this_frame_s`
  says what a stale range does and what that does not catch.
- `src/import.c` — the uploads and the tree walk. Its header says why the order
  is forced, why uploading the pictures has to read the materials first, why the
  walk needs no recursion, and why nothing unwinds on failure.
- `tests/projection.c` — reversed depth, the aspect ratio, and that nothing in
  the projection flips Y. Needs no graphics card.
- `tests/depth_sort.c` — that the order visits the furthest away first, and
  that equal depths keep the order they came in. Its header says why a reversed
  sort is still a correct sort and what the sign looks like on screen. Needs no
  graphics card.
- `tests/normal_matrix.c` — that a normal stays perpendicular to a
  non-uniformly scaled surface and that the world matrix's answer does not. Its
  header says which three cases would pass with the wrong matrix. Needs no
  graphics card.
- `tests/panel.c` — the panel table, and that a panel is sorted among the
  see-through meshes rather than drawn in a pass of its own. Its header says why
  the ordering claim is measured with a draw that is deliberately refused, and
  why both halves of that measurement are needed. The table half needs no
  graphics card; the ordering half skips without one.
- `tests/material.c` — that a material saying nothing about its UV rect reads
  its whole texture, and that one saying something keeps it. Its header says why
  this needs a graphics card to check a decision made on the CPU.
- `tests/import.c` — a hand-built `.glb` in and entities out: the flattening,
  the shared picture, and one frame drawn from what it made. Its header says
  what every number in the file is for and that the mirrored model is what it is
  really looking for.
- `tests/model_data.inc` — that file, as bytes.
