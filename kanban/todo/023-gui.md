## Goal

A GUI that can be drawn in the world or in front of the camera, on a quad.

## Why this was planned for from the very beginning

**This is the case ADR-0024 was written to protect.** A GUI mapped onto a surface
inside the 3D world is the reason nothing in this engine gets a flat screen-space
path, and it is the reason the frame became an offscreen target early. Normally
GUI comes first and 3D is bolted on; here it is deliberately the other way round.

## The question this card was blocked on is answered

**How does a UI quad sit above the world without a separate rendering path?**
Answered by **ADR-0074**, and the answer arrives as card 024 rather than as work
here. In short, and this is all this card needs to know:

- **There is a layer**: an object says whether it belongs to the world or above
  it. Above it means the world is drawn, depth is cleared, and the overlay is
  drawn into the same picture.
- **The overlay is still in 3D and still in metres**, seen through the same
  camera. It is *not* orthographic and *not* screen space — that shape was
  recommended by the tech lead and rejected by the principal, because it is the
  one form a headset could never present and it would have become the easy
  default long before anyone noticed.
- **Both placements are first-class.** A panel on a wall that you walk behind and
  a panel that is always in front are equally ordinary, and neither is the
  exception the other opts out of. **That is the constraint this card most has to
  respect**: a widget system that only really works in one of the two placements
  has broken the decision without anybody editing it.
- **The layer decides order and nothing else.** Whether a panel is lit stays a
  material property, where it already works.
- Inside the layer, blending and sorting are the ordinary rules. Panels with real
  positions sort against each other by distance for free.

## This card builds the panel; it does not inherit one

ADR-0070 decided the glyph atlas for text, on the ground that the offscreen panel
is the atlas plus a render target rather than an alternative to it. So the
offscreen panel is **unbuilt, and it is this card's to build**, for the case that
actually justifies it: a widget tree drawn once and re-used across frames.

Two things follow, and both are already paid for:

- **ADR-0069's premultiplied output is what makes a panel compositable at all.**
  An intermediate target holding non-premultiplied colour cannot be composited
  correctly — its partially covered pixels have already been blended against the
  panel's own background. That decision was taken with this card in mind.
- **Text in a panel is the atlas again**, drawn into the panel's target rather
  than into the frame. Nothing new is needed from `text`.

## Rough scope, in no committed order

- Panels as quads — in world, or in the overlay layer card 024 builds.
- The offscreen panel mechanism: a second render target, a size and resolution
  policy, and the composite back into the frame.
- Hit testing, which needs a ray from the pointer into the world — so it needs
  card 016's input and the camera, and it is genuinely 3D picking rather than 2D
  coordinate comparison. **This is the part that is easy to underestimate**, and
  note that it is picking in *both* placements: an overlay panel has a real
  position in metres too, so there is one mechanism here rather than two.
- Whether the layout model is retained or immediate. A real decision with an ADR,
  not an implementation detail.
- **Text that changes** — D-092. A GUI whose labels are fixed at startup is not a
  GUI, and card 021b builds text once because `voe_render_geometry_create` is a
  startup operation over a pool with no destroy. Either this card solves that or
  a card before it does; it cannot be assumed away. **This is now the card's
  oldest unmet dependency** and the one most likely to be a card of its own.
- **Whether panels want an explicit stacking order** rather than ordering by
  distance. Distance works and is free; *this is in front of that* is what a UI
  author usually means. Card 024 is asked to report whether it feels missing.

## Not in scope, and worth saying now

The editor. It is wanted (ADR-0009) and it is what a GUI eventually serves, but
it is not this card and it is not the next one either.

Any orthographic or screen-space path, which is refused by name and was refused
again on 2026-09-06 when it was the tech lead proposing it.
