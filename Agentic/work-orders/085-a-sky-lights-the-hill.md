# 085 — A sky lights the hill

## What
A scene can have a sky. The sky is drawn behind everything, follows the sun's direction and colour, and
lights the scene from all around, so shaded sides are lit by the blue of the sky and shiny things reflect
it. Far ground fades into a haze, so the valley looks far away. A scene also has an exposure and a tone
curve, so a bright sky and a dark forest both read, and bright light rolls off softly instead of clipping
to white. A scene saved before this feature keeps its look, so the tank and coin games are unchanged.

## Why
The view from the tower is mostly sky and distance. Light from the sky is what makes the hill look
outdoors.

## How to test
1. Open the hill and turn the sky on. The sky shows behind the hill.
2. Turn the sun to low over the horizon. The sky turns to evening colours, and the shaded sides of the hill
   take a warmer, darker light.
3. Turn the sun back up. Shaded slopes are lit softly blue-ish, not black.
4. Fly to the top of the hill. Far ridges fade into haze, nearer ground less so.
5. Place a shiny metal sphere. It reflects the sky.
6. Raise and lower exposure. The picture brightens and darkens, and bright sky rolls off softly.
7. Open the tank game. It looks exactly as before.
8. Open the frame breakdown. The sky is a small share of the frame.
9. Save, close and reopen. Press Play, and ship: the same picture in all three.
